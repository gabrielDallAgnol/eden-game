# Autonomous sims and god games: how technology advances and what the player does

Method note for the report writer. Direct page fetches were blocked in this session (every outside host failed to resolve), so every finding below comes from web-search extracts of the cited page, not from reading the full page. Quotations are the wording the search tool extracted; treat them as close paraphrase unless the page is re-checked. Where the search tool did not say which page a statement came from, that is marked "outlet not pinned". Anything I know only from memory and could not source is in the Gaps lists, never in Cited Findings.

## 1. For each game: who decides what gets researched, the player or the simulation, and what is the trigger?

### Takeaway
In almost every game surveyed the player picks the next technology and a counter pays for it; only the counter's source differs (elapsed time, specialist workers, milestone events, map deposits, population needs). Games where the simulation itself chooses and advances (WorldBox's old culture research, Dwarf Fortress world-history "innovations", Reus villages picking their own projects) are the exception, and none of them carries a civilization from stone to space.

### Cited Findings

#### The Universim (Crytivo; early access 2018, full release 22 January 2024)
- Full release was 22 January 2024 and added "a complete storyline and space exploration". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Universim)
- The player chooses research. Up to five research topics can be queued, with an optional Auto-Research toggle; completed research ("perks") unlocks building types or stat boosts, and some items have prerequisites. — [Official Universim Wiki, Research](https://theuniversim.fandom.com/wiki/Research); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/TheUniversim). Two guides describe the same queue as "four additional items" behind the active one. — [Game8 review](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/the-universim-review); [Superealm guide](https://www.superealm.com/universim-guide-tips-to-get-started/)
- The trigger is elapsed time: research has fixed durations. A Steam-review digest lists "static research times and nonsensical order of the tech tree" as recurring frustrations, and one Steam reviewer describes "waiting 30 minutes for a new tech to research". — [Vaporlens Steam review digest](https://vaporlens.app/app/352720/the_universim); [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/352720/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated)
- Ages named in sources: Stone Age, Medieval Age, Modern Age, Space Age. A player post lists lifespan perks by era (Medical Diagnosis and Antiseptics in the Stone Age, Penicillin in the Medieval Age, Medical Ethics in the Modern Age) and says some perks need "Discovery Points". User-posted, treat as anecdotal. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/352720/discussions/0/3193611175984417898); [Official wiki, Stone Age](https://theuniversim.fandom.com/wiki/Stone_Age)
- A patch note mentions 210 research perks in total after a later update. — [tuxdb patch notes mirror](https://tuxdb.com/game/959&gamenews=1). The 1.0 release added more technology to the tree, including late-game content. — [LadiesGamers review](https://ladiesgamers.com/the-universim-review/)
- Nugget autonomy: nuggets build housing themselves, spreading outward from the Epicenter the player places first; the player cannot choose where houses go. The player places the special buildings (farms, power plants and so on). — [Official wiki, Buildings](https://theuniversim.fandom.com/wiki/Buildings); [NoobFeed review](https://www.noobfeed.com/reviews/the-universim-review-pc); [Big Boss Battle early access review](https://bigbossbattle.com/the-universim-early-access-review-shape-civilisations-from-above/)
- Minute to minute: place special buildings, queue research, spend Creator Points on powers, respond to disasters and animal attacks. One Steam reviewer says the game "wants to automate almost everything, right down to placing buildings without your input" and that the late game "feels more like a resource and worker managing game than a god game". — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/352720/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated)
- End goal: reach the Space Age, build a rocket at the Cosmodrome and colonize other planets (the Moon first). — [Crytivo Space Age devlog part 1](https://crytivo.com/en/the-universim-news/the-universim-space-age-devlog-part-1); [Worthplaying, Moon update, Feb 2022](https://worthplaying.com/article/2022/2/17/news/130738-the-universim-early-access-update-lets-you-colonize-the-moon-trailer/)

#### Dawn of Man (Madruga Works, 2019)
- Six eras in order: Paleolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic, Copper Age, Bronze Age, Iron Age. — [NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/Dawn%20of%20Man); [TheGamer beginner's guide](https://www.thegamer.com/dawn-of-man-beginners-guide/)
- The player chooses every tech, spending knowledge points (KP) in the tech panel. A tech needs its prerequisites and its era unlocked. Every era except the Paleolithic has an "Era Tech" that must be bought before any other tech of that era. — [Dawn of Man Wiki, Research](https://dawnofman.fandom.com/wiki/Research). The Era Tech costs twice as many KP as a normal tech. — [Yekbot guide](https://www.yekbot.com/dawn-of-man-workload-techs-and-work-areas/)
- KP are earned from things that happen in the settlement, not from a research building. Sources list: population growth, clothes crafted, plants farmed, new animals encountered — [PC Gamer preview](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/dawn-of-man-city-builder); hunting (the main source early), then gathering resources later, one point each winter survived, 2 points per completed milestone, 3 points per population step (the first at 10 villagers), and from the Neolithic 2 points per raider event — [Lostnoob, Knowledge points](https://www.lostnoob.com/dawn-of-man/knowledge-points/); building a set number of each structure — [TechRaptor tips](https://techraptor.net/gaming/guides/dawn-of-man-tips-tricks-for-new-players-crops-efficiency-raiders-and-more)
- Tech costs run from 5 to 20 KP. — [Lostnoob](https://www.lostnoob.com/dawn-of-man/knowledge-points/). One guide gives 7 KP per Mesolithic tech and 14 for Pottery. — [Steam guide, Continental Dawn](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1674723684)
- Exploit the counter invites: guides advise building structures in sets of five to earn a KP and then recycling the extras. — [Steamah advanced tips](https://steamah.com/dawn-of-man-advanced-tips-and-tricks/)
- Techs can also be bought from traders, bypassing KP. — [TV Tropes YMMV](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/YMMV/DawnOfMan)
- Entering the Copper Age makes mammoth and woolly rhino go extinct (an era advance changes the world). — [Dawn of Man Wiki, Research](https://dawnofman.fandom.com/wiki/Research)
- Player role: PC Gamer's headline calls it "the ideal city-builder if you hate micromanagement"; villagers carry out tasks themselves under work areas and limits the player sets. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/dawn-of-man-automation-micromanagement)
- End point is the Iron Age; milestones also unlock new maps and modes. There is no further win state in the sources. — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dawn-of-man-beginners-guide/)

#### WorldBox (Maxim Karpenko; sandbox, early access)
- Before version 0.50 the simulation decided. Each culture accumulated "knowledge gain" toward a Research bar; units with high intelligence sped it up; the culture itself picked the next tech, preferring "Priority" techs; "rare" knowledge was capped per culture. Examples of knowledge: roads linking civilizations, military strategy giving an attack bonus. — [Official WorldBox Wiki, Culture Development](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Culture_Development); [NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/WorldBox%20-%20God%20Simulator)
- That system ran out fast: a 2023 Steam thread complains that "after an hour of gameplay the cultures all are at max development". — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/discussions/0/4033600999077871981)
- In 0.50 it was removed. A Steam post states "Cultural Development has been completely removed as of 0.50"; the wiki page is flagged as no longer accurate for 0.50. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1206560/discussions/0/594031317713236425/); [Official wiki](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Culture_Development). A community thread reports the developer said he "removed tech progression because it didn't fit with the new culture mechanic" and would add it back in another update (second-hand report of the developer's words). — [Bugbox suggestion thread, April 2025](https://bugbox.featureupvote.com/suggestions/625953/comment/697922)
- 0.50 replaced it with editable traits; the wiki lists 77 culture traits. Example: in 0.50.3 only xenophobic cultures kill other species and destroy cities, others capture cities. — [Official wiki, Traits](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/Traits); [0.50.3 Xenolith notes](https://the-official-worldbox-wiki.fandom.com/wiki/0.50.3_-_Xenolith)
- Player role: no goals. The player shapes land, spawns races and creatures, and applies disasters and other "God powers" grouped as world creation, civilizations, creatures, nature and disasters, destruction, other. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox)

#### Before We Leave (Balancing Monkey Games, 2020)
- The player chooses techs in the Library and pays with Research. Research is not generated from nothing: the Explorer's Hut produces it from Ancient Technology deposits scattered on each island. — [Before We Leave Wiki, Technology](https://beforeweleave.fandom.com/wiki/Technology); [Ancient Technology](https://beforeweleave.fandom.com/wiki/Ancient_Technology)
- Research comes in colours tied to place. Green is on the starting island; red is on the other islands of the first planet; blue is not on the starting planet at all ("the player must repair the Ruined Spaceship and colonize another Planet to discover Blue Technologies"); purple is only on the innermost and outermost planets. — [Before We Leave Wiki, Ancient Technology](https://beforeweleave.fandom.com/wiki/Ancient_Technology); [Wolf's Gaming Blog review](https://wolfsgamingblog.com/2020/05/21/before-we-leave-review-chilled-city-building-goodness/)
- So the tree is gated by exploration: a higher tier cannot be bought until the settlement has physically reached the place where that colour of ancient technology lies. — [Before We Leave Wiki, Technology](https://beforeweleave.fandom.com/wiki/Technology)

#### Kainga: Seeds of Civilization (Erik Rempen, 1.0 in 2022)
- Technology is found at places on the map. "The Thinker can discover technology at sources of inspiration, scattered around the map." The Thinker is the leader unit; if the Thinker dies the run is lost. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1269710/Kainga_Seeds_of_Civilization/); [Nuuvem listing](https://www.nuuvem.com/item/kainga-seeds-of-civilization)
- The player then chooses among technologies; the store text frames success as "choosing the right technologies that fit your town's need, the environment, challenge and your playstyle". "Your village's culture, technology, and appearance will be unique depending on your environment, the resources available to you and the choices you make." — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1269710/Kainga_Seeds_of_Civilization/)
- Over 140 technologies at early access, above 180 after a later update. Between runs, Karma unlocks new biomes, technologies and Thinkers (roguelite meta-progression). — [Game Developer press release](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/kainga-seeds-of-civilization-new-early-access-content-update-available-now); [Green Man Gaming 1.0 news](https://www.greenmangaming.com/company/news/start-your-journey-in-a-world-full-of-mystery-and-surprise-as-kainga-seeds-of-civilization-v1-0-launches-on-steam-today/)

#### Anno 1800 (Ubisoft Blue Byte, 2019)
- No research bar for the core progression. Five Old World tiers (Farmers, Workers, Artisans, Engineers, Investors) and two New World tiers (Jornaleros, Obreros). — [Anno 1800 Wiki, Population](https://anno1800.fandom.com/wiki/Population)
- Exact mechanism: each residence has basic needs and luxury needs. Fulfilling basic needs raises the number of inhabitants in that house. A house must be full to be upgraded. The upgrade then costs construction materials (timber, bricks, steel beams, windows by tier). Luxury needs give happiness and income and are not required for the upgrade. — [Anno 1800 Wiki, Population](https://anno1800.fandom.com/wiki/Population); [Anno 1800 Wiki, Needs](https://anno1800.fandom.com/wiki/Needs)
- The developers split needs on purpose: one part "relevant for happiness and the other being relevant for the population of your residential buildings", and "it is no longer necessary that your residents are happy in order to advance to the next tier". — [Anno Union DevBlog: Happiness](https://www.anno-union.com/devblog-happiness/)
- What the upgrade unlocks: "if you fulfilled enough needs, residences can be upgraded to unlock the next population tier, as well as new buildings and production chains". — [Anno Union DevBlog: Fulfil Needs Your Way](https://www.anno-union.com/devblog-fulfil-needs-your-way/)
- New buildings appear as the population of a tier passes thresholds (a head-count gate on top of the needs gate). Search extract of a guide, outlet not pinned; consistent with the wiki. — [Anno 1800 Wiki, Population](https://anno1800.fandom.com/wiki/Population)
- Late example of the same pattern: skyscrapers unlock at 5,000 Investors, with elevators produced. — [Anno 1800 Wiki, Skyscrapers](https://anno1800.fandom.com/wiki/Skyscrapers)

#### Black & White (2001) and Black & White 2 (2005)
- B&W1: no tech tree. Villagers worship at the worship site, which produces prayer power for miracles; which miracles exist depends on which villages the god controls ("A god must have control of a town to utilize its miracles"). The village store shows the villagers' desires as flags. — [Black & White Wiki, Worship Site](https://blackandwhite.fandom.com/wiki/Worship_Site); [Black & White Wiki, Miracles](https://blackandwhite.wiki.gg/wiki/Miracles); [Black & White Wiki, Building](https://blackandwhite.fandom.com/wiki/Building)
- B&W1: the area where the god can act grows with population and belief; influence rings start at each temple or village centre. — [Black & White Wiki, Influence](https://blackandwhite.fandom.com/wiki/Influence)
- B&W2: the player buys everything. Tribute "unlocks nearly all content within the game, including new buildings and miracles", and is earned from objectives (for example "build 12 buildings on Impressive areas"). Creature miracles are bought with Tribute, where in the first game the creature learned by repetition. — [Black & White Wiki, Tribute](https://blackandwhite.fandom.com/wiki/Tribute); [Wikipedia, Black & White 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_2)

#### Populous (1989) and Populous: The Beginning (1998)
- Populous: the player's basic act is raising and lowering land to make flat ground; followers build houses on it by themselves; more houses make more followers; more followers raise mana; a higher mana level unlocks more divine powers. — [Giant Bomb, Populous](https://giantbomb.com/populous/3030-6210/)
- Populous: The Beginning: mana is generated by every follower at a rate that depends on follower type and is split 50/50 between charging spells and training followers; new spells and buildings are handed out as rewards for finishing levels. — [Populous Wiki, Mana](https://wiki.popre.net/Mana); [Populous: The Beginning guide](https://ts.popre.net/websites/poptb.com/guide/getting-started/index.html)

#### From Dust (Ubisoft, 2011)
- The player is "The Breath" and can only pick up and drop matter (water, soil, lava). Tribes walk to totems and settle villages there. Settling a totem grants a power (solidify water, put out fires). Some knowledge must be physically carried: a villager walks to a stone, learns for example Repel Water, and walks back to teach it. "Knowledge can be shared between communities, provided you keep a land path open between the two." — [Engadget review](https://www.engadget.com/2011-07-26-from-dust-review.html); [CGMagazine review](https://www.cgmagonline.com/reviews/from-dust/)
- "Less about building a civilization and more about controlling the environment to protect it." — [Game Informer review](https://gameinformer.com/games/from_dust/b/xbox360/archive/2011/07/27/ubisoft%E2%80%99s-god-game-is-a-breath-of-fresh-air)

#### Reus (Abbey Games, 2013)
- The player controls four giants that make biomes and place natural sources (plants, animals, minerals). "Once a natural source is placed by one of the giants, a nomad will appear and establish a village." Only forest, swamp and desert can host villages. — [Steam guide, The Idiot's Guide to Reus](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=225178210); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reus_(video_game))
- Villages develop on their own from what lies inside their borders. Prosperity is the sum of Food, Wealth and Technology in use. Villages start "projects" (basic, improved, grand) with resource requirements the player must meet by arranging sources. Finishing a project yields an ambassador, who rides a giant and unlocks new giant abilities. — [Reus Wiki, Resources](https://wiki.reusgame.com/index.php?title=Resources); [Gamepressure, Projects](https://gamepressure.com/reus/projects/z14fcd); [Gamezebo review](https://www.gamezebo.com/reviews/reus-review/)
- Greed: "Villages increase their Greed if the difference between Resource in Use and Resource available is higher than 20." At medium greed a village wants war with a weaker village; at high greed it attacks the nearest giant. Awe counters it (each point of awe allows one more point of difference); enough Danger also stops greed rising. — [Reus Wiki, Greed](https://wiki.reusgame.com/index.php?title=Greed); [Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=225178210)
- Meta-progression: games run in timed "eras"; completing "developments" (for example reach 150 prosperity in one village using only plants and minerals) unlocks more content for later games. — [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2014/01/29/indie-focus-reus/)

#### Godus (22cans, 2013 early access)
- Loop: sculpt land so followers can build abodes, click the pink belief bubbles the abodes produce, spend belief. "Half your time is spent terraforming, while the other half is clicking frantically around the houses or waiting for more faith." — [The Average Gamer](https://www.theaveragegamer.com/2013/09/18/godus-whats-clicking/); [TechRaptor review](https://techraptor.net/content/review-godus-almost-powerful-deity)

#### Spore (Maxis, 2008), civilization and space stages
- Stage changes are threshold events: the tribal stage begins when the creature's brain is big enough; the space stage begins when the whole planet is united under the player. Many critics treated the first four stages as long tutorials for the space stage. — [Game Developer, Critical Reception](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/critical-reception-ea-maxis-i-spore-i-); [TechRaptor retrospective](https://techraptor.net/content/10-years-of-spore-a-reputation-well-deserved)

#### Crest (Eat Create Sleep, 2018)
- The player writes commandments and has no direct control. "How commandments are interpreted and remembered is not set in stone"; over time they "can develop different associations and completely change their meaning". — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/341710/Crest__an_indirect_god_sim/)
- The more commandments are active, the more confused followers get and the slower progress is. — [GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/k8k0s/its-time-to-play-god-with-crests-new-ancestor-worship-update)
- No technology progression is described in any source found.

#### Briefly
- Frostpunk: player picks; one technology at a time, researched in Workshops, each costing resources; extra workshops speed it up; tree in tiers with branches such as heating and resources. — [Gamepressure, Technologies](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk/technologies/zea19c); [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/frostpunk-research-technology-how/)
- Banished: no tree at all. Store page: "no skill trees"; any structure can be built whenever the materials are gathered. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/242920/Banished); [PC Gamer interview with Luke Hodorowicz](https://pcgamer.com/interview-banished-developer-talks-player-choice-in-the-indie-city-builder-sandbox-game)
- Farthest Frontier: tiered town centre with checklist gates. Tier 2 needs 8 shelters, a market, 30 villagers; tier 3 needs 25 Homesteads, a School, a Trading Post, 150 villagers; tier 4 needs 25 Large Houses, a Theater, a Trading Center, 300 villagers, plus materials and gold. Houses upgrade themselves when needs (food variety, clothes, desirability) are met. — [Farthest Frontier Wiki, Town Center](https://farthestfrontier.wiki/wiki/Town_Center); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1044720/discussions/0/4041482690815194740/)
- Songs of Syx: player picks; knowledge comes from citizens employed in laboratories (boosted by libraries); knowledge is a standing capacity, not a stock: "those points drop when labs stop functioning", so techs can be lost if population or labs fall. — [Songs of Syx Wiki, Lab](https://songsofsyx.com/wiki/index.php/Lab); [Songs of Syx Wiki, Knowledge](https://songsofsyx.com/wiki/index.php/Knowledge); [Reality Remake](https://www.realityremake.com/articles/tag/Songs+of+Syx)
- Dwarf Fortress: the wiki describes no research or unlock step for fortress workshops (that there is no player tech tree is my reading, not a quoted statement). World history does have discoveries: 312 "innovations" that scholars can discover, described by the wiki as "kind of a tech forest/grassland rather than a tree", and innovations tied to practical labours are not in yet (page may be dated). — [Dwarf Fortress Wiki, Topic](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Topic); [Dwarf Fortress Wiki, Workshop](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/Workshop)
- Factorio: player picks; technologies are paid for in science packs, and the last pack type (space science) comes only from rocket launches. — [Factorio Wiki, Space science pack](https://wiki.factorio.com/Space_science_pack)
- Dyson Sphere Program: player picks; the factory automates "matrices" and consumes them for research, which unlocks more advanced matrices. — [NamuWiki, DSP Research](https://en.namu.wiki/w/Dyson%20Sphere%20Program/%EC%97%B0%EA%B5%AC); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyson_Sphere_Program)
- Kerbal Space Program career: player picks; science points come from running experiments in different places (biomes) and are spent on tree nodes that unlock parts. — [KSP forum, design analysis thread](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/105543-career-mode-progression-and-game-design-analysis)
- Timberborn: player picks; Science Points are made by beavers working in the Inventor building and spent to unlock buildings. — [Timberborn Wiki, Science Points](https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Science_Points); [Timberborn Wiki, Inventor](https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Inventor)
- Ymir: 4X plus city builder with pigmen starting in the stone age; "a tech tree of hundreds of technologies"; the current build covers "roughly periods from the Stone Age to the Classical/early medieval Age"; population is divided into social classes with their own incomes and purchasing power. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/378360/Ymir/)
- Rise to Ruins: "godlike village simulator"; the player has spells paid from Influence (a God Wall tile holds 50 influence while it stands and returns it when dispelled). — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/328080/Rise_to_Ruins); [GamePretty advanced techniques](https://gamepretty.com/rise-to-ruins-advanced-techniques/)

### Inferences
- Sorted by who decides: player picks from a tree (Universim, Dawn of Man, Before We Leave, Kainga, Frostpunk, Songs of Syx, Factorio, DSP, KSP, Timberborn, B&W2); progression has no research choice and follows needs or checklists (Anno, Farthest Frontier); simulation decides (WorldBox before 0.50, Dwarf Fortress world history, Reus village projects); no progression (Banished, Crest, WorldBox 0.50).
- The Universim is the only title found that pairs autonomous villagers with a stone-to-space arc, and it uses the least world-connected trigger of all: a timer per perk.
- Games with autonomous people still leave the choice of technology to the player. The two that let the simulation choose (WorldBox, Dwarf Fortress) give the choice no consequence the player must respond to, and WorldBox's version saturated within an hour.
- Dawn of Man's population steps, Anno's population thresholds and Farthest Frontier's villager counts are head-count gates; a design that forbids head-count gates should copy Anno's needs half (house fills because needs are met) and not its threshold half.

### Gaps
- The Universim: I found no source for the exact number of perks per age or the rule that moves the civilization from one age to the next (my recollection is that specific perks or buildings mark the change; unverified).
- The Universim: "Discovery Points" appear in one player post with no explanation of how they are earned.
- Kainga: how many technologies each inspiration source offers and whether the choice is from a random hand could not be confirmed (my recollection is a small random selection per inspiration; unverified).
- Dwarf Fortress: I could not source the developer's stated reason for having no tech tree, nor the often-repeated "nothing past about the year 1400" rule, nor an explicit statement that all workshops are buildable from the start; all three are from memory and unverified.
- Rise to Ruins, Ymir: no source found for how their technology or building unlocks are triggered.
- WorldBox: whether technology progression has returned after 0.50 is unconfirmed; a fan changelog lists version 0.51.2 with "knowledge" items for equipment storage, which suggests something exists ([fan changelog](https://superworldbox.com/changelog)).
- Black & White 1 food, wood and breeder mechanics and Godus's card and sticker system were not covered by any source found.

## 2. Which games tie an advance to something that happened in the world rather than to a points bar?

### Takeaway
Five mechanisms tie advances to the world: place (Before We Leave, Kainga, From Dust), deeds (Dawn of Man), felt needs (Anno, Farthest Frontier houses), surroundings (Reus), and population-generated power (Populous, Black & White 1). All but From Dust and Reus still convert the event into a number the player spends.

### Cited Findings
- Place, resource found: Before We Leave research exists only where Ancient Technology deposits are, and each tier's colour lies on a farther island or planet, so the next tier cannot be researched until someone has got there. — [Before We Leave Wiki, Ancient Technology](https://beforeweleave.fandom.com/wiki/Ancient_Technology)
- Place, thing witnessed: Kainga's Thinker "can discover technology at sources of inspiration, scattered around the map"; culture and technology depend on "your environment, the resources available to you". — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1269710/Kainga_Seeds_of_Civilization/)
- Place, knowledge carried by a person: in From Dust a villager must walk to the knowledge stone and walk back, and knowledge spreads between villages only along an open land path. — [Engadget review](https://www.engadget.com/2011-07-26-from-dust-review.html)
- Deeds: Dawn of Man gives knowledge for first-time and cumulative deeds: new animals encountered, hunting, gathering, clothes crafted, plants farmed, buildings built, winters survived, raids. The points are then pooled and spent on anything. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/dawn-of-man-city-builder); [Lostnoob](https://www.lostnoob.com/dawn-of-man/knowledge-points/)
- Felt needs: Anno 1800 houses fill only when basic needs are supplied, and only a full house can be upgraded to the tier that brings new needs, buildings and production chains. — [Anno 1800 Wiki, Population](https://anno1800.fandom.com/wiki/Population); [Anno Union DevBlog](https://www.anno-union.com/devblog-fulfil-needs-your-way/)
- Felt needs, automatic: Farthest Frontier houses upgrade on their own when food variety, clothing and desirability are met (with auto-upgrade on). — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1044720/discussions/0/3757726983069082112)
- Surroundings: Reus villages grow and pick projects according to the sources inside their borders; village type (forest favours food, desert wealth, swamp science) follows the biome. — [Gamepressure, Civilizations](https://www.gamepressure.com/reus/civilizations/zf4fcb); [Reus Wiki, Resources](https://wiki.reusgame.com/index.php?title=Resources)
- People generate the power: in Populous, powers unlock as the follower count (and so mana level) rises; in Black & White 1, miracles are available according to the towns held and are paid for by villagers actually worshipping. — [Giant Bomb, Populous](https://giantbomb.com/populous/3030-6210/); [Black & White Wiki, Worship Site](https://blackandwhite.fandom.com/wiki/Worship_Site)
- World reacts to an advance: Dawn of Man's Copper Age removes mammoths and woolly rhinos. — [Dawn of Man Wiki, Research](https://dawnofman.fandom.com/wiki/Research)
- Knowledge needs upkeep: Songs of Syx knowledge falls when laboratories stop working, so a famine or plague can cost technology. — [Songs of Syx Wiki, Lab](https://songsofsyx.com/wiki/index.php/Lab)
- Counter-examples that are pure bars: The Universim (timer per perk), Timberborn (Inventor output), Frostpunk (workshop time plus resources), B&W2 (Tribute). — [Universim Wiki, Research](https://theuniversim.fandom.com/wiki/Research); [Timberborn Wiki](https://timberborn.wiki.gg/wiki/Science_Points); [Gamepressure, Frostpunk](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk/technologies/zea19c); [Black & White Wiki, Tribute](https://blackandwhite.fandom.com/wiki/Tribute)

### Inferences
- No game found links a specific event to a specific discovery (for example "saw lightning burn a tree, so fire"). Dawn of Man comes closest in spirit, but its deeds only fill a general pool, which is why players farm it by building five huts and tearing them down.
- A pooled currency is what makes farming possible. If each deed could only ever unlock the thing it is about, the build-five-and-recycle exploit would have no payoff.
- From Dust and Before We Leave show that "someone must go there and come back" is an understandable, visible cause that needs no meter.
- Anno shows that needs can drive a whole progression with no research at all, but Anno's player builds every supply chain by hand; no source shows the needs-driven model running with autonomous villagers except Farthest Frontier's self-upgrading houses.

### Gaps
- I found no developer statement (GDC talk or blog) from any of these studios explaining why they chose pooled points over event-specific discoveries.
- Whether Kainga's inspirations are tied to what the Thinker is looking at (a specific plant, animal or landmark) or are generic nodes could not be confirmed.

## 3. In god games with autonomous people, what gives the player a purpose over tens of hours? Which powers exist, what do they cost, and how are new powers unlocked?

### Takeaway
Purpose comes from one of four things: the people need the god to do something they cannot (shape land in Populous, Godus, From Dust, Reus), the god's power is earned from the people (belief, mana, prayer), the people can turn on the god or each other (Reus greed), or the player sets their own goals (WorldBox). Where the god's work is optional help on top of a self-sufficient city (The Universim), players report feeling like a manager.

### Cited Findings
- The Universim powers and currency: powers cost Creator Points (CP); "you accumulate CP at a certain rate and each Creator Power costs a varying amount", scaled to the action; early-development example: throwing stones, trees or people cost 60 CP (numbers likely changed since). — [ModDB developer post, Creator Powers](https://www.moddb.com/news/creator-powers-god-powers/)
- The Universim: CP are "generated over time and by your Nuggets' faith"; regeneration depends on population faith, and "fear-based Faith ... does generate Creator Points twice as fast as love-based Faith". Believers are won by showing power in front of nuggets, for example telekinesis in a crowd. — [Ludo.guide](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/the-universim/getting-started/divine-powers-creator-points); [ModDB developer post, Have a Little Faith](https://www.moddb.com/news/have-a-little-faith); [GamesFuze guide](https://gamesfuze.com/?p=1100)
- The Universim: reviewers call the powers the highlight, "ranging from simple levitation to wrathful storms"; one Steam user found them "too powerful/cheap, which is fun but also feels weird". — [Game8 review](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/the-universim-review); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/352720/discussions/0/4139438683073856881)
- The Universim: once followers are hard to gain late on, a Steam reviewer says it "feels more like a resource and worker managing game than a god game". — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/352720/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated)
- Populous: the god's necessary job is flat land; power grows with the number of followers, and higher mana unlocks further powers. — [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/populous/3030-6210/)
- Black & White 1: belief converts neutral villages and widens the area where the god can act; villagers "become bored with repetitive attempts to impress them", which forces varied methods; a trained creature can convert distant villages. — [Giant Bomb, Black & White](https://www.giantbomb.com/wd/3030-18623); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game))
- Black & White 1: PC Gamer's retrospective says the game treats being a god as "more about influence than micromanagement", and also that there is "a tremendous amount of fat". Metacritic 90. — [PC Gamer, Reinstall](https://pcgamer.com/reinstall-black-white); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_%26_White_(video_game))
- Black & White 2: powers and buildings are bought with Tribute earned from objectives. — [Black & White Wiki, Tribute](https://blackandwhite.fandom.com/wiki/Tribute)
- From Dust: powers are temporary grants from settled totems; the purpose is protecting villages from tsunami, lava and fire while the tribe migrates. — [Engadget review](https://www.engadget.com/2011-07-26-from-dust-review.html)
- Reus: the god's gifts are the only source of village resources, and over-giving is punished: growth faster than the village can absorb raises greed, greedy villages attack neighbours and then the giants. New giant abilities come from ambassadors earned by completed village projects, and different village types give different ambassadors. — [Reus Wiki, Greed](https://wiki.reusgame.com/index.php?title=Greed); [Gamezebo review](https://www.gamezebo.com/reviews/reus-review/); [Gamepressure, Projects](https://gamepressure.com/reus/projects/z14fcd)
- Crest: the only power is wording commandments; the limit is that more active commandments confuse followers. — [GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/k8k0s/its-time-to-play-god-with-crests-new-ancestor-worship-update)
- Rise to Ruins: powers draw on a pool of Influence; standing spells such as God Wall reserve part of the maximum while they exist. — [GamePretty](https://gamepretty.com/rise-to-ruins-advanced-techniques/)
- WorldBox: no source found describes any in-game cost for powers; the free version unlocks only part of the power list and a premium payment unlocks the rest; there are no objectives. Rock Paper Shotgun's headline: "WorldBox is a god sim too smart to give you goals"; the reviewer added "I'd probably had my fill of WorldBox after around 4 hours, but it was a happy four hours". — [Wikipedia, citing Graham Smith, RPS, 8 Jan 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldBox); [NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/WorldBox%20-%20God%20Simulator)

### Inferences
- Three working ways to unlock powers appear: by number of believers (Populous), by which settlements exist and what they have achieved (B&W1 town miracles, Reus ambassadors, From Dust totems), and by buying (B&W2 Tribute, Universim CP). The second ties the god's growth to the people's growth and is the best match for a village that advances itself.
- Reus is the clearest worked example of "helping too much is a mistake": the punishment is a rate limit (gap between resources in use and available above 20), softened by awe. It makes the god's question "how fast" and not just "what".
- A free-powers sandbox holds a professional reviewer for about four hours; a costed-powers game with a goal is needed for tens of hours.
- In The Universim the people do not need the god for anything except speed and rescue, and the people do not push back; both of Reus's hooks are missing.

### Gaps
- Current (1.0) Creator Point costs for individual Universim powers and how new powers are unlocked there (research perks or belief level) were not found.
- I could not source Black & White 1's cost table for miracles or how the good/evil alignment changes them.
- Godus's power list and unlock system (cards and stickers) were not found in any source.
- Populous: The Beginning exact per-spell mana costs were not found.

## 4. What are the documented criticisms: boredom at high speed, the player feeling unnecessary, late-game drag?

### Takeaway
The repeated complaint about self-running god games is waiting: research on fixed timers with nothing to do in between (The Universim), clicking to collect (Godus), or running out of things within hours (WorldBox, Spore's stages). Games with no progression are criticised for the opposite reason: no sense of advancement (Banished).

### Cited Findings
- The Universim, critic: GameStar (70 on Metacritic) says the game starts off great and then flattens out to the point of boredom. — [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/the-universim/)
- The Universim, early access preview: "all my civilizations hit the point where all I really had to do was more research, but there was nothing for me to do in-between the research". — [Christ Centered Gamer preview](https://www.christcenteredgamer.com/reviews/pc-mac/the-universim-preview-pc)
- The Universim, 1.0 review: 74/100, "a charming little God game where being a God actually matters"; pacing issues with long waits for research and construction. — [Game8 review](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/the-universim-review)
- The Universim, 1.0 review: lists "slow-burn pacing and a disorganized research tree" as weaknesses. — [KeenGamer review](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/pc-reviews/the-universim-review-creative-incarnate-pc/). Another 7.5/10 review says in-game speed and tutorial still need tuning. — [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/16105/the-universim/reviews). Another says the opening is slow with few villagers but worth the wait. — [Unboxed Reviews](https://unboxedreviews.com/the-universim-review/)
- The Universim, players: review digest reports 68% positive sentiment, with the loop called "repetitive, tedious, and slow-paced, especially in the mid-to-late game" and performance degrading with large cities. Individual reviews: "you can basically see and do everything that this game has to offer within the first ten or so hours"; the endgame economy "falls apart just about as the game is ending"; no option to take manual control of residential placement; houses are prioritised over every other building "regardless of divine intervention". — [Vaporlens digest](https://vaporlens.app/app/352720/the_universim); [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/352720/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated); [NoobFeed review](https://www.noobfeed.com/reviews/the-universim-review-pc)
- The Universim, thread titles: "I want to Love it, but its sooo boring!" and "This game sucks, TO SLOW." — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/352720/discussions/0/1743352529782056805/); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/352720/discussions/2/2260186248418350768/)
- The Universim, counter-view: Metacritic PC user reviews are 73% positive; one user reports two years of play without burning out, calling it "epic in scope, yet entirely accessible and relaxing". — [Metacritic user reviews](https://www.metacritic.com/game/the-universim/user-reviews/)
- Dawn of Man: knowledge points are slow to come in the early game (player thread); the building-count milestones are farmed by building and recycling. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/858810/discussions/0/3581993464535559632); [Steamah](https://steamah.com/dawn-of-man-advanced-tips-and-tricks/)

- Before We Leave: repairing the spaceship "takes a lot of resources, and this is where one of the game's deepest flaws rears its ugly head — the pacing is awful" (search extract, outlet not pinned; among the results were [TechRaptor preview](https://techraptor.net/gaming/previews/before-we-leave-preview), titled "Promising Design and Frustrating Flaws", and [Save or Quit preview](https://saveorquit.com/2020/05/16/preview-before-we-leave/)).
- Kainga: mixed reviews (OpenCritic scores of 6/10 and 6.5/10); complaints of weak AI, bugs, no save, poor tutorial, random outcomes; one review is titled "Bumper Crop Of Failure". — [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/14137/kainga-seeds-of-civilization); [GameLuster review](https://gameluster.com/review-kainga-seeds-of-civilization-bumper-crop-of-failure/); [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/kainga-seeds-of-civilization/)
- Godus: "you will do a ton of clicking. A TON of clicking"; "there's really not much to do in the early hours beyond clicking and waiting". — [TechRaptor review](https://techraptor.net/content/review-godus-almost-powerful-deity); [The Average Gamer](https://www.theaveragegamer.com/2013/09/18/godus-whats-clicking/)
- Spore: "both the Tribal Stage and the Civilisation Stage are by far the weakest parts"; the civilization stage is "a pared down version of Civilisation"; in space "grinding missions for cash to expand my empire was exhausting". - Banished: NPR criticised the lack of technology progression: "there's no sense of advancement through the game". — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2014/02/21/280776282/banished-is-like-simcity-without-the-city-but-with-cholera)

### Inferences
- The Universim's central failure, as reported, is a fixed research clock in a town that runs itself: the player has done everything useful and must wait. Fast-forward then becomes the main control, and reviewers call the result boredom.
- Autonomy cuts both ways: reviewers praise self-placed houses for looking lived-in, then complain when autonomous priorities (houses first) override what the god wants and there is no way to object.
- When autonomous people fail visibly and stupidly (From Dust, Rise to Ruins), the player blames the AI, not their own play. Crest's players made the further point that they could not see why followers did what they did.
- Every pooled-points system surveyed has a documented way to farm it or outrun it (Dawn of Man building recycling, KSP biome hopping, WorldBox max culture in an hour).

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the text of any Rock Paper Shotgun, PC Gamer, Eurogamer or IGN review of The Universim 1.0; it is unclear whether those outlets reviewed the full release. Evidence here is from smaller outlets, GameStar's Metacritic blurb and Steam players.
- No quantified data (median playtime, share of players reaching the Space Age via achievements) was found for The Universim.
- Eurogamer's Godus review and reviews of Reus that criticise its repetition were not retrieved.
- No reviews discussing boredom specifically at high game speed were found for games other than The Universim.

## 5. Which games end with a rocket or space launch as the goal, and what is the chain of requirements?

### Takeaway
Factorio is the clean model: one launch is the win, and the whole factory exists to feed it. The Universim and Before We Leave treat the launch as a gateway to more of the same game on other planets, and both are criticised for the grind around it.

### Cited Findings
- The Universim chain: advance through the Modern Age; build the Observatory, which "will be a starting point for the Space Age and will ultimately unlock the Space Map to locate new planets" and receives signals and distress calls; send exploration satellites to assess planets; build the Cosmodrome, where colonization rockets are built and loaded with supplies; only one rocket can be built per planet. (2021 devlog; details may differ in 1.0.) — [Crytivo Space Age devlog part 1](https://crytivo.com/en/the-universim-news/the-universim-space-age-devlog-part-1)
- The Universim after launch: the Moon was the first target (February 2022 update); it has no water and no life; nuggets first build domed houses for oxygen. Other planet types: dead worlds rich in resources including rare "Unobtanium", lava planets needing special technology, and planets with friendly or hostile aliens. The launch is a continuation, not an ending. — [Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2022/2/17/news/130738-the-universim-early-access-update-lets-you-colonize-the-moon-trailer/); [Crytivo devlog](https://crytivo.com/en/the-universim-news/the-universim-space-age-devlog-part-1); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Universim)
- Before We Leave chain: gather green research on the first island; settle further islands for red research; red research unlocks space travel; repair the Ruined Spaceship found on the home planet (very resource-heavy), or later build a launch pad for new ships; other planets hold blue and purple research. — [Before We Leave Wiki, Ancient Technology](https://beforeweleave.fandom.com/wiki/Ancient_Technology); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1073910/discussions/0/5079478487781420842); [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/before-we-leave-review/)
- Factorio: "the rocket silo is currently the only win condition". Launching a rocket, empty or with any payload, shows the victory screen; a satellite is no longer required to win. Chain: research the rocket silo (needs every science pack type except military); craft 100 rocket parts in the silo (50 with the Space Age expansion); each part needs 10 low density structures, 10 rocket fuel and 10 rocket control units (another extract names processing units as the third input, which may reflect a different version). A launch carrying a satellite returns 1,000 space science packs for further, infinite research. — [Factorio Wiki, Rocket silo](https://wiki.factorio.com/Rocket_silo); [Factorio Wiki, Space science pack](https://wiki.factorio.com/Space_science_pack)
- Dyson Sphere Program: the goal is the sphere; "factory expansion and research continues until components for the Dyson sphere are finally constructed and launched into their target orbit" from launch structures such as the Vertical Launching Silo; the top-tier matrix feeds infinite research. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyson_Sphere_Program); [DSP Wiki, Vertical Launching Silo](https://dyson-sphere-program.fandom.com/wiki/Vertical_Launching_Silo)
- Kerbal Space Program career: launching is the starting activity, not the goal; the tree is exhausted with "a few percent" of available science and nothing marks an ending. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/220200/discussions/0/3044985412476903598)
- Spore: space is the final, open-ended stage, reached by uniting the planet. — [Game Developer, Critical Reception](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/critical-reception-ea-maxis-i-spore-i-)
- No space ending: Dawn of Man stops at the Iron Age; Ymir's current build stops at the classical or early medieval period; Anno 1800, Banished, Farthest Frontier, Songs of Syx, Timberborn, WorldBox, Reus, Crest, Black & White, Populous, From Dust, Godus and Rise to Ruins have no launch goal in any source found. — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dawn-of-man-beginners-guide/); [Ymir Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/378360/Ymir/)

### Inferences
- Only The Universim attempts the full stone-age-to-space arc with autonomous people, so there is one direct precedent and its reception is mixed.
- Factorio's launch works as an ending because the requirement is a quantity of made things (100 parts, each from three intermediate goods) that every earlier system feeds; the player can see the whole chain and how far along it is. The Universim's requirement is a building reached by waiting through timed research.
- Both games that continue after the launch (The Universim, Before We Leave) restart the settlement loop on a new body with a harsher constraint (no oxygen, no water), which reviewers and players describe as slow or repetitive. A single launch as the end avoids that.

- The resource costs of The Universim's Cosmodrome and rocket in 1.0 and the exact research perks required were not found.
- Factorio's exact science-pack list for the rocket silo technology in version 2.0 was inconsistent across search extracts; check the Factorio wiki page "Rocket silo (research)" before quoting it.
- Whether The Universim 1.0's storyline has a defined ending after colonizing other planets was not found.
- Before We Leave's exact spaceship repair costs were not found.
