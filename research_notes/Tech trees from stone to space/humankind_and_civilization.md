## 1. Humankind: eras, technology counts, prerequisites, era stars, the Neolithic, end conditions

### Takeaway
Humankind has seven eras, the first of which (Neolithic) has no research at all: you wander as a tribe and need one Era Star to become a civilisation; every later step needs seven Era Stars, and stars also pay out the Fame that decides the winner. The tech tree has roughly 90 technologies in six era blocks, a technology can only be researched once the empire has reached its era, and finishing the tree or launching a Mars mission ends the game without necessarily winning it.

### Cited Findings

**Eras and era advancement**
- Seven eras: Neolithic, Ancient, Classical, Medieval, Early Modern, Industrial, Contemporary — [PC Invasion beginner guide](https://www.pcinvasion.com/humankind-beginners-guide-neolithic-era/2/); [Green Man Gaming](https://www.greenmangaming.com/blog/humankind-eras/)
- The game starts as a nomadic tribe in the Neolithic; the player must earn an Era Star to move to the Ancient Era and pick a first culture — [PC Gamer getting-started guide](https://www.pcgamer.com/humankind-guide-tips/)
- There are three Neolithic objectives and completing any one lets you advance; the Growth Star needs five population / five units, obtained mostly by picking up food "curiosities" on the map — [PC Invasion](https://www.pcinvasion.com/humankind-beginners-guide-neolithic-era/2/)
- Neolithic play is exploration: covering ground to find curiosities, sanctuaries (pillaging one takes a turn and gives food that spawns more units) and sites for outposts, while fighting wild animals whose numbers decline over time — [PC Invasion](https://www.pcinvasion.com/humankind-beginners-guide-neolithic-era/2/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/humankind-guide-tips/)
- Neolithic to Ancient needs only one Era Star; every later transition needs at least 7 Era Stars — [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/humankind/ancient-era-how-to-advance/z0ec35); [Humankind wiki, Era Star](https://humankind.fandom.com/wiki/Era_Star)
- Era Stars are grouped by the seven culture affinities: Aesthete, Agrarian, Builder, Expansionist, Merchant, Militarist, Scientist (the Scientist stars are earned by researching a number of technologies) — [Humankind wiki, Era Star](https://humankind.fandom.com/wiki/Era_Star); [PC Gamer on Fame](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/humankind-fame-era-stars)
- Advancing is a choice, not automatic: stars not yet earned in the old era are forfeited, so players often stay to collect more Fame before moving on — [PC Gamer on Fame](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/humankind-fame-era-stars)
- A forum post claims up to 13 stars can be collected per era — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1124300/discussions/0/3092275748064029470) (unreliable; see Gaps for the figure of 21 recalled by the researcher)

**Technology counts and gating**
- "Technologies can only be researched within an Era, except for Scientist cultures who can research technologies in the next Era as well" — [Humankind wiki, Technology](https://humankind.fandom.com/wiki/Technology)
- Within the eras open to you, you do not need every technology of an era to research later ones (example given: skipping Sailing in the Ancient era and coming back for it) — [Amplitude forum, OpenDev first impressions](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/humankind/forums/208-lucy-opendev/threads/38012-first-impressions-technology-future-eras-and-other-ideas?page=1) (player report)
- Per-era counts from one fan wiki: Ancient 13, Classical 12, Medieval 10, Early Modern 11, Industrial 17, i.e. 63 up to the Industrial era (no Contemporary figure) — [NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/HUMANKIND/%EA%B8%B0%EC%88%A0)
- Robert Zubek's analysis counts 12 Ancient and 12 Classical technologies "with similar numbers in later eras" — [Zubek, "Humankind and the rhetoric of tech trees"](https://robert.zubek.net/blog/example-humankind); the Ancient figure conflicts with NamuWiki's 13
- The fan ruleset [HK-fan] carries 91 Humankind-named technologies: Ancient 12, Classical 12, Medieval 10, Early Modern 11, Industrial 18, Contemporary 28 (same 63 total through Industrial as NamuWiki, split 12/18 instead of 13/17) — [Kurwizimi/Humankind-Cultures, jsons/Techs.json](https://github.com/Kurwizimi/Humankind-Cultures)
- The first four Ancient technologies are Calendar, Domestication, Carpentry and City Defense — [Neoseeker technology guide](https://www.neoseeker.com/humankind/Technology_Guide)
- Zubek's main observation: compared with Civ V, Humankind's tree is weighted much more heavily towards warfare — [CivFanatics summary of Zubek](https://civfanatics.com/2022/02/21/robert-zubek-humankind-and-the-rhetoric-of-tech-trees/)

**End of game**
- Humankind has one victory condition: the most Fame when the game ends. Ending the game and winning it are separate; the empire that triggers the end is not necessarily the winner — [GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/humankind-victory-conditions-end-fame); [GamerTweak](https://gamertweak.com/humankind-win-conditions)
- Default end triggers as listed by guides: reaching the last turn; one empire earning every Contemporary Era Star; one empire completing the technology tree; eliminating or vassalising all other empires; or making the world unfit for human life (pollution). One guide lists the Mars mission only under the separate "Space Race" preset; the researcher recalls it also being part of the default set (not verified) — [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/humankind-end-conditions-how-change/); [GamerTweak](https://gamertweak.com/end-game-humankind)
- Turn limits by game pace: Blitz 75, Fast 150, Normal 300, Slow 450, Endless 600 — [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/humankind-end-conditions-how-change/)
- A "Space Race" end-condition preset ends the game only on the last turn, a Mars mission, or uninhabitable world; the Mars route is: complete the "Mars Colony" national project, then launch the mission — [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/humankind-end-conditions-how-change/); [GamerTweak](https://gamertweak.com/end-game-humankind)
- Players disliked "researching every tech ends the game" enough that a mod removes it (NoTechLimit) — [Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/humankind/mods/4); the condition was debated on the official forum during OpenDev — [Amplitude forum](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/humankind/forums/206-opendev/threads/44044-endgame-techs-end-the-game-condition?page=1)
- Players also reported the tree being hard to finish within the turn limit — [Amplitude forum, technology pacing](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/humankind/forums/168-general/threads/46716-the-real-problem-with-technology-pacing-maximus-patch?page=1)

### Inferences
- Humankind separates three things that Civilization bundles together: *what you may research* (gated by the era you have reached), *when you reach the next era* (gated by Era Stars, i.e. by deeds in the world: population, districts, money, kills, territory, influence, techs), and *who wins* (Fame). Research alone therefore cannot drive a player through history; you cannot beeline from Bronze Working to Gunpowder because the Early Modern block is closed until seven stars have been earned twice more.
- The Neolithic is the purest "causes, not timers" phase in any of these games: there is no research queue, and progress out of it comes from things found and done on the map (food found, animals hunted, knowledge found).
- The tree thickens sharply late: about 10 to 12 technologies in each of the first four eras, then roughly 18 and 28. The late eras carry most of the content.
- The "completing the tree ends the game" rule was unpopular; it turns the last technology into a doomsday clock instead of a reward.

### Gaps
- An authoritative list with Amplitude's own prerequisites and science costs could not be fetched (the official Humankind Encyclopedia at humankind-encyclopedia.games2gether.com and the wiki were blocked). The total is "about 90"; 91 in the fan ruleset; treat any exact total as unverified.
- Recalled, not verified: the three Neolithic stars are Growth (reach 5 units), Hunter (kill 5 animals) and Knowledge (collect 10 science from curiosities); each of the seven affinities offers 3 stars per era for 21 available per era; the Scientist affinity's ability is what allows research one era ahead.
- Recalled, not verified: the Contemporary space chain is three national projects in order (an artificial satellite, a lunar landing, then the Mars colony), each a multi-stage shared construction project unlocked by late Contemporary technologies. A 2021 Steam thread reported the Mars Colony project failing to end a Space Race game, which suggests a bug at that time — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1124300/discussions/0/2963922521564951652).
- No developer interview or post-mortem on the Humankind tree was found.

## 2. Civilization V, VI and VII: size of the tree, eras, layout, the Civics tree, Eurekas

### Takeaway
Civ V (80 techs in Gods & Kings, 81 in Brave New World) and Civ VI (68 in base/Rise and Fall, with nine more in Gathering Storm) both draw one left-to-right lattice of eight or nine eras in which each technology has one to three prerequisites from neighbouring rows; Civ VI splits off a second, culture-driven Civics tree and attaches to almost every node a "boost" worth 40% of its cost (50% at original release) earned by doing a related thing on the map. Civ VII abandons the single tree: three ages, each with its own small tree of about 15 technologies that is thrown away at the age transition.

**The Civics tree (Civ VI)**
- A second tree, researched with culture instead of science and running in parallel with the tech tree. 51 civics in base + Rise and Fall: Ancient 7, Classical 7, Medieval 7, Renaissance 6, Industrial 7, Modern 9, Atomic 5, Information 3 (incl. repeatable Future Civic). Costs run from 20 culture (Code of Laws, the single root) to 3200 (Future Civic) — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Civics unlock governments, policy cards, some districts and wonders; the tech tree unlocks units, buildings, improvements and most districts — [Fandom, "Breaking Down the Civilization VI Tech Tree"](https://www.fandom.com/articles/breaking-civilization-vi-tech-tree) (page title only seen; district split confirmed in [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts))
- The two trees are knitted together by boosts in both directions. Tech boosted by a civic: Stirrups ("Have the Feudalism civic"), Banking (Guilds), Scientific Theory (The Enlightenment), Robotics (Globalization). Civic boosted by a tech: Games and Recreation ("Research the Construction technology"), Colonialism (Astronomy), Mass Media (Radio), Cold War (Nuclear Fission), Social Media (Telecommunications); the Space Race civic is boosted by building a Spaceport — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)

**Eureka (technology) and Inspiration (civic) boosts**
- A boost gives an immediate lump of progress towards one technology when a specific goal is met; the tech tree screen shows each tech's goal; boosts can also come from Great People, research agreements and espionage — [Civilopedia, Gathering Storm](https://www.civilopedia.net/gathering-storm/concepts/science_3)
- Size: every boost row in the Rise and Fall database has `Boost = 40`, i.e. 40% of the cost, for both technologies and civics — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts). At original release it was 50% ("50% boost, triggered by having 1 Quarry") — [CivFanatics Civ VI technologies](https://civfanatics.com/civ6/info/technology/)
- Coverage: 64 of 68 technologies have a boost entry (all except Pottery, Animal Husbandry, Mining and Future Tech) and 48 of 51 civics have an Inspiration — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Six late technologies have no world trigger and read "Boost through Great Scientist or Spy": Rocketry, Nuclear Fission, Satellites, Lasers, Stealth Technology, Nuclear Fusion (internal class `NONE_LATE_GAME_CRITICAL_TECH`) — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- The triggers fall into a small number of kinds (internal class names): have X improvements; improve a specific resource; own X units of a type; kill with a unit type; kill X barbarians; construct a building; have X buildings; have X districts; have X different specialty districts; settle on the coast; find a natural wonder; meet a civilisation; maintain X trade routes; earn a Great Person; hold a civic; have a government with X policy slots; build a wonder of an era or later; extract an artifact; create a national park; reach an alliance level — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Eureka triggers, exact in-game wording (technology -> trigger) — [Civ6-DB localisation](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts):
  - Ancient: Sailing -> Found a city on the Coast. Astrology -> Find a natural wonder. Irrigation -> Farm a resource. Writing -> Meet another civilization. Archery -> Kill a unit with a Slinger. Masonry -> Build a Quarry. Bronze Working -> Kill 3 Barbarians. The Wheel -> Mine a resource.
  - Classical: Celestial Navigation -> Improve 2 sea resources. Currency -> Make a Trade Route. Horseback Riding -> Build a Pasture. Iron Working -> Build an Iron Mine. Shipbuilding -> Own 2 Galleys. Mathematics -> Build 3 different specialty districts. Construction -> Build a Water Mill. Engineering -> Build Ancient Walls.

- Modern, 16 + Future Tech: Academics, Steam Engine, Military Science 1400 (three roots); Electricity, Urbanization, Combustion, Industrialization 2425; Radio, Flight, Mass Production 3000; Computation, Mobilization, Armor 4000; Aerodynamics 6000; Rocketry, Nuclear Fission 8500; Future Tech 10000 — [Civ7-data](https://github.com/mateicanavra/civ7-official-resources)
- Shape: a narrow braid. Each column has three or four techs and each tech needs one or two from the column before (for example Feudalism <- Cartography, Astronomy; Shipbuilding <- Feudalism, Heraldry), narrowing to one or two capstones and then Future Tech. In the Modern tree Aerodynamics needs all three of Computation, Mobilization and Armor, a full pinch point before Rocketry and Nuclear Fission — [Civ7-data](https://github.com/mateicanavra/civ7-official-resources)
- Each technology has a second tier (a "mastery"): unlocks are tagged depth 1 or depth 2, and some space projects sit at depth 2 (for example Trans-Oceanic Flight at Flight depth 2) — [Civ7-data](https://github.com/mateicanavra/civ7-official-resources)
- Civ VII has no Eureka boosts in the data examined (no boost table in the tech files) — [Civ7-data](https://github.com/mateicanavra/civ7-official-resources); treat as "not found", not as proven absent

### Inferences
- Both Civ V and Civ VI price technology by column (era half), not by content, with cost multiplying by roughly 1.5 to 2 per step early and flattening to 1.1 to 1.3 per step late. Civ V's last column costs 440 times its first; Civ VI's about 86 times.
- Civ VI's tree is far looser than Civ V's: about 1.3 prerequisites per tech and long near-independent rows, against Civ V where most mid-game techs take two or three prerequisites that tie neighbouring rows together.
- The Eureka system is the nearest thing in any of these games to "a thing happens because the means were at hand": the world state (a quarry exists, a city sits on the coast, three barbarians are dead) pushes a specific piece of knowledge forward. But it is only a discount. The player still chooses what to research from a menu and science points still pay for it. Firaxis also cut the discount from 50% to 40% in Rise and Fall, and gave the six "critical" late technologies (rocketry, fission, satellites, lasers, stealth, fusion) no world trigger at all.
- The trigger vocabulary is small and reusable: about twenty kinds of observation of the world ("own N of X", "built X next to Y", "killed with X", "met someone", "found something") cover 100+ boosts.
- Civ VI boosts frequently point at the *precursor in practice* of the knowledge: Iron Working from an iron mine, Rifling from a niter mine, Steel from a coal mine plus an ironclad, Plastics from an oil well, Astronomy from a university beside a mountain, Sailing from a coastal city, Celestial Navigation from working sea resources.
- Civ VII's three small trees with hard resets trade away long-range planning (no beelining from the Bronze Age to gunpowder) for three short, complete arcs.

### Gaps
- Gathering Storm exact totals could not be read from a primary source. Recalled, not verified: 77 technologies (the 68 above plus Buttress, Refining, Seasteads, Advanced AI, Advanced Power Cells, Cybernetics, Smart Materials, Predictive Systems, Offworld Mission) and about 61 civics.
- Brave New World's exact tree could not be read; the lists above are Gods & Kings. Recalled, not verified: BNW keeps the same shape, adds The Internet, and moves the reveal of Iron from Iron Working to Bronze Working.
- Recalled, not verified: in Civ V an era is "entered" the moment the first technology of that era is learned; there is no gate between eras in Civ V or Civ VI other than prerequisites.
- No developer interview on the design intent of Eurekas was retrieved. A CivFanatics article "Soren Johnson's designer notes about the tech tree" exists but could not be opened — [CivFanatics](https://civfanatics.com/?p=5683).
- Whether Civ VII has any boost-like mechanic, and its launch-version (February 2025) tech lists, were not verified; the snapshot is from October 2026.

## 3. The science / space-race victory: steps, technologies, buildings, and the chain back to the start

### Takeaway
In every Civilization game the space race is a short ladder of very expensive production projects, each gated by one late technology and by a dedicated launch facility, and the first rung is always Rocketry. Civ VI's first launch can be reached having researched only 25 of 67 technologies (a quarter of the tree's science cost), but finishing the race forces almost the whole tree (60 of 67).

### Cited Findings

**Civilization V (Gods & Kings data)**
- Victory definition: build the Apollo Program, then build all spaceship parts and add them to the spaceship in the capital. Required parts: 3 SS Booster, 1 SS Cockpit, 1 SS Stasis Chamber, 1 SS Engine — [Unciv-G&K VictoryTypes.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/VictoryTypes.json)
- Apollo Program: a national project, 750 production, requires Rocketry, cannot be hurried, triggers a global alert, "enables construction of Spaceship parts" — [Unciv-G&K Buildings.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/Buildings.json)
- Part technologies: SS Booster <- Advanced Ballistics; SS Cockpit <- Satellites; SS Stasis Chamber <- Nanotechnology; SS Engine <- Particle Physics. Each part is listed as requiring Aluminum — [Unciv-G&K Units.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/Units.json)
- Supporting buildings: Spaceship Factory (requires Robotics and Aluminum, 360 production, +50% production of spaceship parts in the city); Hubble Space Telescope wonder (requires Satellites; 2 free Great Scientists, +25% spaceship part production, free Spaceship Factory) — [Unciv-G&K Buildings.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/Buildings.json)
- Chain to Rocketry: Rocketry <- Radar <- Ballistics + Electronics. Ballistics <- Flight <- Steam Power <- Industrialization + Scientific Theory + Rifling. Electronics <- Replaceable Parts (<- Electricity + Steam Power) + Flight. Continuing down, Rocketry needs 46 of the 79 non-repeatable techs, costing 47,175 of 202,600 total science (23%) — computed from [Unciv-G&K Techs.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/Techs.json)
- Technologies up to the Atomic era that are *not* needed for Rocketry: Sailing, Optics, Compass, Astronomy, Navigation (the whole sea line); Chemistry, Fertilizer, Military Science, Dynamite, Railroads, Combustion, Combined Arms; Archaeology, Biology, Refrigeration, Radio, Plastics, Pharmaceuticals, Atomic Theory, Ecology, Nuclear Fission, Computers — computed from [Unciv-G&K Techs.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/Techs.json)
- All five spaceship technologies together (Rocketry, Advanced Ballistics, Satellites, Particle Physics, Nanotechnology) require 75 of 80 techs, 83% of total science cost; only Lasers, Globalization, Nuclear Fusion, Stealth and Future Tech can be left out — computed from [Unciv-G&K Techs.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/Techs.json)

**Civilization VI, base game and Rise and Fall**
- The Spaceport is a district unlocked by Rocketry with a base production cost of 1800 (every ordinary district is 54 at base) — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Five space-race projects, all built in a city with a Spaceport, one of each per player — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts):
  1. Launch Earth Satellite — requires Rocketry — 900 production
  2. Launch Moon Landing — requires Satellites, and Launch Earth Satellite completed — 1500 production
  3. Launch Mars Reactor — requires Nuclear Fusion, and Moon Landing completed — 1800 production
  4. Launch Mars Habitation — requires Robotics, and Moon Landing completed — 1800 production
  5. Launch Mars Hydroponics — requires Nanotechnology, and Moon Landing completed — 1800 production
- The three Mars modules can be built in any order, in parallel at different Spaceports, once the Moon Landing is done — [Civ6-DB ProjectPrereqs](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts); [Civ VI wiki, Victory](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Victory_(Civ6))
- Chain to Rocketry (1410 science): Rocketry <- Chemistry + Radio. Chemistry <- Sanitation <- Scientific Theory <- Astronomy + Banking. Astronomy <- Education <- Apprenticeship + Mathematics. Banking <- Education + Stirrups. Radio <- Flight + Steam Power. Flight <- Industrialization + Scientific Theory. Steam Power <- Industrialization <- Mass Production + Square Rigging. Mass Production <- Education + Shipbuilding. Square Rigging <- Cartography <- Shipbuilding <- Sailing. Roots: Pottery -> Writing -> Currency -> Mathematics; Animal Husbandry -> Archery -> Horseback Riding -> Stirrups; Sailing — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- That is 25 technologies including Rocketry, costing 13,540 science, 25% of the tree's total. Not needed for the first launch: Mining and everything that depends on it (Masonry, Bronze Working, The Wheel, Iron Working, Construction, Engineering, Machinery, Military Engineering, Castles, Gunpowder, Printing, Metal Casting, Siege Tactics, Ballistics, Military Science, Rifling, Economics, Replaceable Parts, Steel, Combustion), plus Astrology, Celestial Navigation, Irrigation, Military Tactics, Electricity — computed from [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- All five space technologies together (Rocketry, Satellites, Robotics, Nuclear Fusion, Nanotechnology) require 60 of 67 non-repeatable techs, 48,610 science, 89% of the tree. Only Astrology, Celestial Navigation, Irrigation, Military Tactics, Guidance Systems, Stealth Technology and Telecommunications can be skipped — computed from [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- The three Mars technologies sit at the ends of three different rows: Robotics (top, computing: Electricity -> Computers -> Robotics), Nuclear Fusion (military-nuclear: Steel/Combustion -> Combined Arms + Advanced Ballistics -> Nuclear Fission -> Lasers -> Nuclear Fusion), Nanotechnology (materials: Combustion -> Plastics -> Synthetic Materials -> Composites -> Nanotechnology) — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)

**Civilization VI, Gathering Storm**
- Five steps. The first two (Launch Earth Satellite, Launch Moon Landing) are unchanged; the three Mars modules are merged into a single Mars colony project; two new steps follow: launch an Exoplanet Expedition, then the expedition must travel to the exoplanet. Completing the Exoplanet Expedition project starts the journey automatically and the victory comes on arrival — [Civ VI wiki, Victory](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Victory_(Civ6))
- The Exoplanet Expedition requires the Smart Materials technology — [Screen Rant science victory guide](https://screenrant.com/science-victory-guide-civ6-civilization/)
- The journey is 50 light years ("Help the Exoplanet Expedition travel 50 light years") — [Civ VI wiki, Victory](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Victory_(Civ6))
- Two further Spaceport projects, Terrestrial Laser Station and Lagrange Laser Station, speed the journey — [Epic Games Store science victory guide](https://store.epicgames.com/news/civilization-6-guide-to-a-science-victory)
- The Space Race civic's Inspiration is "Build a Spaceport district" — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)

**Civilization VII**
- At launch the Modern-age Science legacy path ("Space Race") was three projects, each worth one legacy point: Trans-Oceanic Flight (needs an Aerodrome), Break the Sound Barrier (needs Aerodynamics), Launch Satellite (needs a Launch Pad); completing the path unlocks the victory project First Staffed Space Flight, built in a city with a Launch Pad — [PC Gamer victory guide](https://pcgamer.com/games/strategy/civilization-7-victory-legacy-paths-win-conditions-guide); [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/how-to-get-a-civilization-vii-science-victory/); [2K official victories page](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-guide/gameplay/victories/)
- Firaxis later reworked this: "at launch, Civ VII required the completion of three Modern Age Projects to launch the rocket and win", after which projects were spread across every age and now give "Innovation", the currency that wins the Science Victory — [2K dev diary, Victories post-launch](https://civilization.2k.com/ko-KR/civ-vii/game-guide/dev-diary/victories-post-launch)
- October 2026 data: Trans-Oceanic Flight (1000 production, needs an Airfield building, unlocked by Flight mastery, grants a Squadron Commander and 5 Innovation); Break the Sound Barrier (1000, Airfield, unlocked by Aerodynamics mastery, +1 combat strength to air units and 5 Innovation); Launch Satellite (1000, Launch Pad, unlocked by Rocketry, reveals the whole map and 5 Innovation); Radio Telescope (1200, needs a Radio Station, unlocked by Radio mastery, 5 Innovation); Crewed Space Flight Preparation (2000, Launch Pad, Rocketry, 15 Innovation, repeatable); Crewed Space Flight (3000, Launch Pad, requires the Science legacy path complete, "launch the first person into space and win a Scientific Victory"; cheaper for each scientific legacy point earned earlier in the campaign) — [Civ7-data, age-modern](https://github.com/mateicanavra/civ7-official-resources)
- Buildings: Airfield <- Flight; Launch Pad <- Rocketry; Radio Station <- Radio; Laboratory <- Electricity; Factory <- Mass Production; Rail Station <- Industrialization; Manhattan Project wonder <- Nuclear Fission — [Civ7-data](https://github.com/mateicanavra/civ7-official-resources)
- Chain to Rocketry inside the Modern tree: Rocketry <- Aerodynamics <- Computation + Mobilization + Armor; Computation <- Radio <- Electricity + Urbanization; Mobilization <- Flight <- Urbanization + Combustion; Armor <- Mass Production <- Combustion + Industrialization; roots Academics, Steam Engine, Military Science. Rocketry therefore needs all 14 other Modern technologies; only Nuclear Fission is off the path — [Civ7-data](https://github.com/mateicanavra/civ7-official-resources)
- Civ VII's race ends at the first crewed space flight, not at the Moon, Mars or another star — [Civ7-data](https://github.com/mateicanavra/civ7-official-resources)

**Humankind**
- A Mars mission is an end-of-game trigger, not a victory: complete the "Mars Colony" national project and send the mission; the winner is still whoever has the most Fame — [GamerTweak](https://gamertweak.com/end-game-humankind); [GameWatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/humankind-victory-conditions-end-fame)

### Inferences
- The common pattern: (1) one technology named Rocketry opens a dedicated, very expensive launch site (Civ VI Spaceport at 33 times a normal district; Civ V Apollo Program; Civ VII Launch Pad); (2) three to six projects in a fixed order, each needing one more late technology; (3) the projects cost production, not science, so an empire needs industry as well as research; (4) the last rung needs technologies from the ends of otherwise unrelated branches, which forces the whole tree to be filled in.
- Real history's ladder is preserved in miniature: powered flight -> radio/radar -> rocketry -> satellite -> crewed flight / Moon -> Mars -> another star. Civ VII makes the aviation rungs explicit (ocean crossing, sound barrier) before the satellite.
- Firaxis keeps lengthening the tail: Civ V stops at a colony ship launch, Civ VI vanilla at a Mars colony, Gathering Storm at an exoplanet 50 light years away with a travel timer; Civ VII went the other way and stops at first crewed flight.
- Rocketry in Civ VI descends from chemistry (sanitation, scientific theory, astronomy, education) and from radio (flight, steam power, industrialisation, shipbuilding). It does not descend from gunpowder, metal casting or ballistics at all. In Civ V it descends from ballistics and electronics via radar. Neither tree ties rocketry to nitre or to metallurgy.

### Gaps
- Gathering Storm specifics not verified from a primary source. Recalled, not verified: the single Mars project is "Launch Mars Colony" and requires Nanotechnology; the expedition travels 1 light year per turn at base; Terrestrial Laser Station and Lagrange Laser Station require the Offworld Mission technology, are repeatable, and each completed one adds 1 light year per turn; the laser stations and late projects consume Power and Aluminum.
- Whether Civ V spaceship parts truly require Aluminum in Firaxis's own data (as the Unciv ruleset states) was not cross-checked; the Spaceship Factory's Aluminum requirement is well known.
- Humankind's exact space projects, their order and the technologies that unlock them could not be sourced (see section 1 Gaps).

## 4. Branches a player can prioritise, how real the choice is, and where branches re-converge

### Takeaway
The choice of path is real in the short and middle term and illusory in the long term: a player can ignore the sea, or iron and siege, or religion, for several eras, but the late game is built so that capstone technologies need the ends of every branch. Humankind and Civ VII cap how far ahead any branch can run (era gate, age reset); Civ V and VI allow deep beelines.

### Cited Findings
- Civ VI rows and the themes they carry (see section 2): sea and later electricity/computing; astrology and industry/flight; the science spine (Mathematics -> Education -> Astronomy -> Scientific Theory -> Sanitation -> Chemistry -> Rocketry -> Satellites); economy (Pottery -> Writing -> Currency -> Apprenticeship -> Banking -> Economics); horse/gun/atom; construction/engines/materials; metal and siege; wheel and machinery — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Civ VI: a whole root can be skipped for a long time. Launch Earth Satellite is reachable without ever researching Mining, and so without Bronze Working, Iron Working, The Wheel, Masonry or anything below them; 25 techs suffice out of 67 — computed from [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Civ VI re-convergence points: Future Tech needs Nanotechnology + Nuclear Fusion + Robotics + Satellites; the Mars modules need three of those; cross-links between rows before that include Construction (Horseback Riding + Masonry), Apprenticeship (Currency + Horseback Riding), Machinery (Engineering + Iron Working), Gunpowder (Apprenticeship + Military Engineering + Stirrups), Industrialization (Mass Production + Square Rigging), Flight (Industrialization + Scientific Theory), Economics (Metal Casting + Scientific Theory), Rocketry (Chemistry + Radio) — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Civ VI: fully finishing the space race needs 60 of 67 techs (89% of cost); the only branches that stay optional to the end are religion's root (Astrology, Celestial Navigation), Irrigation, Military Tactics, and three Information-era military techs — computed from [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Civ V: the sea line (Sailing, Optics, Compass, Astronomy, Navigation) and the explosives/engine line (Chemistry, Fertilizer, Dynamite, Railroads, Combustion) are skippable all the way to Rocketry, which needs 46 of 79 techs; all spaceship parts need 75 of 80 — computed from [Unciv-G&K Techs.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/Techs.json)
- Civ V knits rows together much earlier than Civ VI: Civil Service needs Currency + Horseback Riding + Drama and Poetry; Printing Press needs Machinery + Physics + Chivalry; Steam Power needs Industrialization + Scientific Theory + Rifling — [Unciv-G&K Techs.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/Techs.json)
- Civ V's own data weights branches for its computer players: +100% weight on Pottery, Mining, Calendar, Sailing, The Wheel, Currency, Construction, Engineering, Civil Service, Metal Casting, and later on science/economy techs; -50% on Archery, Iron Working, Compass, Steel, Gunpowder, Metallurgy, Rifling, Combustion — i.e. the game itself treats growth-and-economy first as the sound default and the war line as the detour — [Unciv-G&K Techs.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/Techs.json) (these weights are Unciv's AI hints, not confirmed as Firaxis values)
- Humankind: a branch cannot outrun the era. Technologies of a later era are closed until the empire reaches it, with a one-era exception for Scientist cultures — [Humankind wiki, Technology](https://humankind.fandom.com/wiki/Technology). Inside an era a branch can be left for later (ships skipped in the Ancient era and picked up afterwards) — [Amplitude forum](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/humankind/forums/208-lucy-opendev/threads/38012-first-impressions-technology-future-eras-and-other-ideas?page=1)
- Humankind's tree leans to warfare more than Civ V's — [CivFanatics on Zubek](https://civfanatics.com/2022/02/21/robert-zubek-humankind-and-the-rhetoric-of-tech-trees/)
- Civ VII: each age's tree is five or six columns of two to four technologies with one or two prerequisites per node, so "branching" is a matter of order within an age; the Modern tree pinches completely at Aerodynamics (needs Computation + Mobilization + Armor) — [Civ7-data](https://github.com/mateicanavra/civ7-official-resources)
- Civ VI adds a different kind of branch choice: which tree to feed. Science goes to technologies, culture to civics, and several districts, all governments and all policy cards are only on the civics side — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)

### Inferences
- Four devices control how real a branch choice is: (a) number of prerequisites per node (Civ VI about 1.3: loose; Civ V nearer 2: tight); (b) capstones that take the tips of several branches (Future Tech, the spaceship parts); (c) an outer gate independent of research (Humankind's Era Stars, Civ VII's age end); (d) discounts that reward what you are already doing (Eurekas), which make the branch that matches your land and behaviour cheaper without closing the others.
- Branch identity in all these trees is the same handful of themes: water (boats, harbours, navigation); food and growth; stone and building; metal and weapons; animals and mounted war; writing, learning and science; trade and money; faith and culture (moved to the Civics tree in Civ VI).
- Because of device (b), "skipping" is really "deferring" in Civ. The only technologies that stay permanently optional are leaf nodes that nothing depends on (Civ VI: Military Tactics, Guidance Systems, Stealth, Telecommunications; Civ V: Lasers, Stealth, Globalization).
- A landlocked Civ VI player still has to research Sailing, Shipbuilding, Cartography and Square Rigging to industrialise (Industrialization <- Mass Production + Square Rigging). That is a case where the lattice overrides what the land would suggest.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of how often players actually beeline, or developer statements on intended branch freedom, was retrieved.
- Humankind's real prerequisite graph was not available, so its re-convergence points cannot be listed.

## 5. How resources on the land map to technologies

### Takeaway
All three series use the same two-part rule: a strategic resource is invisible on the map until a "reveal" technology is known, and then a different, usually later, technology unlocks the unit or building that consumes it. The reveal technology is often one step before the use, and Civ VI ties the Eureka for the use technology to actually working the deposit.

### Cited Findings

**Civilization VI (base + Rise and Fall)**
- Reveal technology per strategic resource: Iron <- Bronze Working; Niter <- Military Engineering; Coal <- Industrialization; Oil <- Steel; Aluminum <- Radio; Uranium <- Combined Arms; Horses have no reveal technology in this database — [Civ6-DB Resources table](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Units that require a resource, and the technology that unlocks the unit: Swordsman (Iron; Iron Working), Horseman (Horses; Horseback Riding), Knight (Iron; Stirrups), Musketman (Niter; Gunpowder), Bombard (Niter; Metal Casting), Cavalry (Horses; Military Science), Ironclad (Coal; Steam Power), Battleship (Coal; Steel), Tank (Oil; Combustion), Fighter and Bomber (Aluminum; Advanced Flight), Aircraft Carrier (Oil; Combined Arms), Nuclear Submarine (Uranium; Telecommunications), Jet Fighter (Aluminum; Lasers), Jet Bomber (Aluminum; Stealth Technology), Modern Armor (Uranium; Composites) — [Civ6-DB Units table](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Reveal comes before use: iron is seen at Bronze Working (80 science) and used at Iron Working (120); niter seen at Military Engineering (390), used at Gunpowder (540); oil seen at Steel (1140), but the Oil Well improvement needs Combustion (1250) and the Offshore Oil Rig needs Plastics (1410) — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Improvements that work resources are themselves technology-gated: Mine and Quarry <- Mining; Pasture and Camp <- Animal Husbandry; Fishing Boats <- Sailing; Plantation <- Irrigation; Lumber Mill <- Machinery — [Civ6-DB Improvements table](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Working the deposit pushes the follow-on technology: Iron Working Eureka = build an Iron Mine; Rifling = build a Niter Mine; Steel = build a Coal Mine and an Ironclad; Plastics = build an Oil Well; Nanotechnology = build an Aluminum Mine; and generically The Wheel = mine a resource, Irrigation = farm a resource, Horseback Riding = build a Pasture, Masonry = build a Quarry, Celestial Navigation = improve 2 sea resources — [Civ6-DB](https://github.com/kebnoa/Civ6Scripts)
- Gathering Storm changes: Oil is revealed by Refining; improved strategic resources yield an amount per turn (+3 Oil per turn for an improved source) into a stockpile with a cap, raised by Encampment buildings; units need a lump of the resource to be built, and some need upkeep each turn (a Tank needs 1 Oil per turn; without it units fight weaker and cannot heal); power plants burn Coal, Oil or Uranium to make electricity — [Civilopedia, Oil (Gathering Storm)](https://civilopedia.net/gathering-storm/resources/resource_oil); [Gamepressure, strategic resources in Gathering Storm](https://www.gamepressure.com/sidmeierscivilization6/strategic-resources-gs/zfbf52); [Civilopedia, consumable resources](https://www.civilopedia.net/gathering-storm/concepts/consumeable_resources)

**Civilization V (Gods & Kings data)**
- Reveal technology: Horses <- Animal Husbandry; Iron <- Iron Working; Coal <- Industrialization; Oil <- Biology; Aluminum <- Electricity; Uranium <- Atomic Theory — [Unciv-G&K TileResources.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/TileResources.json)
- Consumers and their unlock technology: Chariot Archer (Horses; The Wheel), Horseman (Horses; Horseback Riding), Swordsman (Iron; Iron Working), Knight (Horses; Chivalry), Longswordsman (Iron; Steel), Frigate (Iron; Navigation), Lancer (Horses; Metallurgy), Cavalry (Horses; Military Science), Ironclad (Coal; Steam Power), early aircraft (Oil; Flight), Battleship (Oil; Electronics), Tank-line (Oil; Combustion, Combined Arms), later aircraft (Oil; Radar), Atomic Bomb (Uranium; Nuclear Fission), Rocket Artillery (Aluminum; Rocketry), Nuclear Missile (consumes 2 Uranium; Advanced Ballistics), Helicopter Gunship (Aluminum; Computers), Jet Fighter (Aluminum; Lasers), Stealth Bomber (Aluminum; Stealth), Giant Death Robot (Uranium; Nuclear Fusion), Spaceship Factory and spaceship parts (Aluminum) — [Unciv-G&K Units.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/Units.json); [Buildings.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/Buildings.json)
- Reveal and use are again separated: oil is revealed by a life-science technology (Biology, cost 2350) one column before anything burns it (Flight, 3100); aluminium is revealed by Electricity (2350) and first consumed at Rocketry (6400); uranium is revealed at Atomic Theory and consumed at Nuclear Fission — [Unciv-G&K](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/tree/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings)
- In Civ V each deposit supplies a fixed number of copies and each unit holds one copy while it exists — implied by the ruleset's "Consumes [2] [Uranium]" and `requiredResource` fields — [Unciv-G&K Units.json](https://github.com/yairm210/Unciv/blob/master/android/assets/jsons/Civ%20V%20-%20Gods%20%26%20Kings/Units.json)

**Humankind**
- Strategic resources are limited in number; deposits stay hidden until the matching technology is researched; a city or outpost in the same territory exploits one by building a Resource Extractor on the tile; copies can also be bought from other empires by trade with the same effect — [Gamepressure, Humankind strategic resources](https://www.gamepressure.com/humankind/strategic-resources/z3fa48); [Humankind wiki, Strategic Resource](https://humankind.fandom.com/wiki/Strategic_Resource)
- The set runs from copper and horses to coal and uranium — [Cultured Vultures resources guide](https://culturedvultures.com/?p=162384). Horses are used by all cavalry (scouts, horsemen, knights, hussars); copper by Ancient units (spearmen, chariots, war elephants) and again by later mortars; iron from the Medieval era onwards; uranium by modern tanks, nuclear submarines, missile cruisers and nuclear weapons — [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/humankind/strategic-resources/z3fa48)
- Units need a number of copies of a resource, so one deposit may not be enough: a player lacking a second saltpetre could not build strong ships or musketeers, and a developer floated a later technology that would generate saltpetre — [Amplitude forum, "Saltpetre problem"](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/humankind/ideas/3003-saltpetre-problem)
- The fan ruleset maps the eight resources to reveal technologies as: Horses <- Domestication; Copper <- Bronze Working; Iron <- Standing Army; Saltpetre <- Gunpowder Warfare; Coal <- Steam Engine; Oil <- Combustion Engine; Aluminium <- Aerial Warfare; Uranium <- Uranium Enrichment — [HK-fan TileResources.json](https://github.com/Kurwizimi/Humankind-Cultures) (a fan adaptation; the real game's reveal technologies may differ)

**Civilization VII**
- Resources are assigned to ages rather than revealed by technologies: Coal is listed only for the Modern age; Horses for all three; Iron for at least Antiquity and Exploration. Coal and Horses are of class "Empire" (an empire-wide bonus when held, +1 production on the tile) rather than a unit prerequisite — [Civ7-data resource tables](https://github.com/mateicanavra/civ7-official-resources)

### Inferences
- The standard sequence is: reveal technology -> improvement technology -> consumer technology, usually spread over one to three columns. It creates a mid-game scramble: you learn where the coal is before you can use it, and may have to settle, trade or fight for it.
- The reveal technology is chosen thematically, not causally, and sometimes oddly: Civ V reveals oil with Biology and aluminium with Electricity; Civ VI reveals iron with Bronze Working and uranium with Combined Arms. In none of these games does finding or handling the material itself reveal it. Knowledge always comes first and the land second, the reverse of Civ VI's Eureka logic, which makes the two systems in Civ VI pull in opposite directions (you cannot build the iron mine that boosts Iron Working until Bronze Working has shown you the iron).
- Three accounting models exist: Civ V and Humankind count *copies* (a deposit gives N, a unit holds some while alive); Civ VI before Gathering Storm is a threshold (recalled, not verified: have 2 sources, or 1 in a city with an Encampment); Gathering Storm is a *flow into a stockpile* with build costs and per-turn upkeep, the only one where fuel is actually burned.
- The resource list is identical across the Civ games and nearly so in Humankind: horses, (copper), iron, nitre/saltpetre, coal, oil, aluminium, uranium, which maps one-to-one onto military generations (cavalry, swords, guns, steamships, tanks, aircraft, nuclear).

### Gaps
- Civ VI lists no reveal technology for Horses in the March 2018 database; the researcher recalls the game hiding horses until Animal Husbandry. Unresolved conflict.
- Brave New World is recalled (not verified) to reveal Iron at Bronze Working instead of Iron Working, and Factories in Civ V are recalled to require Coal.
- Humankind's real reveal technologies and copy counts per unit were not available from a primary source.
- Civ VII: the role of "Empire", "City", "Bonus", "Treasure" and "Factory" resource classes was not investigated beyond the three resources above.

## 6. Empire Earth and Age of Empires: advancing through ages

### Takeaway
Both real-time series make advancing an age a single paid purchase at the town centre, allowed only after the player has put up a couple of ordinary buildings in the current age. There is no research tree to climb between ages: the age is the gate, and technologies are separate purchases at individual buildings within it.

### Cited Findings
- Age of Empires II, official tutorial: advancing is bought at the Town Center. Dark Age to Feudal Age needs two Dark Age buildings (the tutorial names a Lumber Camp and a Mill) and 500 food; Feudal to Castle needs two Feudal Age buildings (a Blacksmith and a Stable in the tutorial; any two of Archery Range, Stable, Blacksmith, Market per Liquipedia) and 800 food + 200 gold; Castle to Imperial costs 1,000 food + 800 gold and needs Castle Age buildings — [ageofempires.com, "What is Advancing?"](https://www.ageofempires.com/learn-to-play/advancing-aoe2/); [Liquipedia, Castle Age](https://liquipedia.net/ageofempires/Castle_Age); [Gamer Journalist](https://gamerjournalist.com/age-of-empires-2-how-to-advance-ages/)
- Requirements and costs can vary by civilisation and by game version (the original Age of Kings manual lists a Mill and a Barracks for the Feudal Age) — [Age of Kings manual (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/382441561/AoK-Manual-pdf)
- Empire Earth has 14 epochs in the base game and 15 with The Art of Conquest expansion, from the Prehistoric Age to the Space Age; the first four are Prehistoric, Stone, Copper and Bronze — [Wikipedia, Empire Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Earth_(video_game)); [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/254760)
- Empire Earth: advancing to the next epoch requires the construction of two recruitment or technology buildings in the current epoch (walls, gates, towers and houses do not count) and then spending a quantity of resources; the cost rises with each epoch — [Empire Earth wiki, Epoch](https://empireearth.fandom.com/wiki/Epoch); [Empire Earth manual (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/390634269/Empire-Earth-Manual-PC-pdf); [DVD Talk forum](https://forum.dvdtalk.com/video-game-talk/196435-empire-earth-questions.html)
- Empire Earth II replaced this with research: each epoch offers 12 technologies and 6 of them must be researched to advance; resources also come and go with the epochs (iron is first available in epoch 4 and no longer used after epoch 9) — [Empire Earth wiki, Technology (Empire Earth II)](https://empireearth.fandom.com/wiki/Technology_(Empire_Earth_II))

### Inferences
- The RTS model is the opposite pole from Civ VI's lattice: one linear track of ages, a visible price, and a building prerequisite that proves the player has actually used the current age. The building prerequisite is deliberately loose (any two of several), so it reads as "have a functioning settlement of this age" rather than a recipe.
- Empire Earth II's "research any 6 of 12 to advance" is a middle ground worth noting: breadth of knowledge in the current age is the gate, the player chooses which half, and nothing is permanently lost because the other six remain available.
- Empire Earth is the only game here that runs from before the stone age (Prehistoric epoch) past the present (Nano Age, Space Age), with 14 to 15 steps against Civ's 8 to 9 eras and Humankind's 7.
- In all three RTS designs the age-up costs food first and gold later (500 food; 800 food + 200 gold; 1,000 food + 800 gold in AoE II), i.e. early advancement is paid for by surplus food and later advancement by trade or mined wealth.

### Gaps
- Exact Empire Earth epoch-up prices per epoch were not found; the manual and wiki pages could not be opened.
- Recalled, not verified: the full Empire Earth epoch list is Prehistoric, Stone, Copper, Bronze, Dark, Middle Ages, Renaissance, Imperial, Industrial, Atomic (World War I), Atomic (World War II), Atomic (Modern), Digital, Nano, plus Space in the expansion; the epoch-up is bought at the Town Center / Capitol.
- Recalled, not verified: the first Age of Empires has four ages (Stone, Tool, Bronze, Iron) bought the same way; Age of Empires IV replaces the generic purchase with building one of two "landmark" buildings per age, each with a different bonus.
